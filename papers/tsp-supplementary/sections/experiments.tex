\subsection{Controlled Nonlinear Tensors}
\label{supp:controlled-details}

The controlled tensors in Fig.~\ref{paper-fig:controlled-alpha} have size
$10\times10\times10$ and Tucker rank $(4,4,4)$. For each of ten seeds, all
entries of the core and factors are drawn independently from $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$.
Suppressing the seed index, we form
\begin{equation}
  \cS=\tucker{\cX}{\bA^{(1)},\bA^{(2)},\bA^{(3)}}.
  \label{eq:supp-controlled-signal}
\end{equation}
We then rescale $\cS$ to unit root-mean-square value, keeping the same notation.
The nonlinear panels apply one of the following scalar functions entrywise:
\begin{equation}
  g(s) \in
  \left\{
  s^2+s^3,\;
  \tanh(\kappa_{\tanh}s),\;
  \frac{e^{\kappa_{\exp}s}-1}{\kappa_{\exp}}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:supp-controlled-responses}
\end{equation}
Writing $q=q_{0.9}(|\cS|)+10^{-8}$, where $q_{0.9}$ is the empirical
90th percentile, we set $\kappa_{\tanh}=1.5/q$ and $\kappa_{\exp}=1/q$.

To isolate the nonlinear part, we remove the component of $g(\cS)$
parallel to $\cS$:
\begin{equation}
  \cR=g(\cS)
  -\frac{\inner{g(\cS)}{\cS}}
  {\norm{\cS}_F^2+10^{-8}}\cS.
  \label{eq:supp-controlled-residual}
\end{equation}
No intercept is included in this projection. We also rescale $\cR$ to unit
root-mean-square value, again keeping the same notation. Consequently,
$\cS$ and $\cR$ are orthogonal and have the same energy. The clean
nonlinear measurement is
\begin{equation}
  \cY_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{clean}}
  =\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,\cS
  +\sqrt{\alpha}\,\cR.
  \label{eq:supp-controlled-mixture}
\end{equation}
We use $\alpha\in\{0.10,0.15,\ldots,0.40\}$, so $\alpha$ is exactly the energy
fraction assigned to the nonlinear residual. The identity panel instead uses
$\cY_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{clean}}=\cS$ for every $\alpha$.

Finally,
\begin{equation}
  \cY_{\alpha}=\cY_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{clean}}+\cE,
  \qquad
  E_{\mathbf i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,10^{-3}),
  \label{eq:supp-controlled-noise}
\end{equation}
which gives 30~dB SNR because every clean tensor has unit root-mean-square
value. Signal seeds are $0,\ldots,9$; noise seeds are deterministically derived
from the signal seed, response, and $\alpha$ in the released experiment script.

All $1000$ entries are used for both fitting and evaluation. Tucker uses rank
$(4,4,4)$, SVD initialization, at most 1000 HOOI iterations, and tolerance
$10^{-4}$. The matched parameter choices are CP rank 6 and TT rank
$(1,3,3,1)$. CP uses random initialization with the corresponding signal seed,
at most 1000 ALS
iterations, and tolerance $10^{-8}$; TT uses the standard TT-SVD. NTD-PL uses
Tucker rank $(4,4,4)$ and $P=6$ in the $\alpha$ sweep. It starts from a Tucker
fit with at most 50 iterations and then runs 1000 Adam iterations with core and
factor learning rates $10^{-4}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$. The core, factor, and
response regularization weights are all $10^{-6}$, the
response coefficients are refreshed every five iterations, and polynomial
degrees are activated by continuation. These settings give 184, 186, 150,
and 191 parameters for Tucker, CP, TT, and NTD-PL, respectively.

Figure~\ref{fig:supp-controlled-degree} fixes $\alpha=0.25$ and refits NTD-PL
for every $P\in\{1,\ldots,6\}$ using the same ten seeds. Figure~\ref{fig:supp-controlled-traces}
also fixes $\alpha=0.25$ and $P=6$; its curves are the mean RMSE over the ten
seeds, with shaded bands showing one standard deviation.

\subsection{Metrics and Normalization}
\label{supp:metrics}

For a test index set $\Gamma$, the RMSE is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}
  =
  \left(
  |\Gamma|^{-1}
  \sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\Gamma}
  (Y_{\mathbf{i}}-\widehat{Y}_{\mathbf{i}})^2
  \right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For completion, $\Gamma$ contains the entries hidden during fitting. For
reconstruction, $\Gamma$ contains all tensor entries. We also report
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{NMSE}
    &=
    \frac{
      \sum_{\mathbf i\in\Gamma}
      (Y_{\mathbf i}-\widehat Y_{\mathbf i})^2
    }{
      \sum_{\mathbf i\in\Gamma}Y_{\mathbf i}^2
    },\\
    \mathrm{NMSE(dB)}
    &=10\log_{10}(\mathrm{NMSE}).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{paper-tab:cave-main-baselines} computes NMSE(dB) separately for
each CAVE scene and then reports the mean and standard deviation over the 15
scene values. Spectral angle mapper
is computed pixelwise on spectral vectors,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SAM}(\mathbf{y},\widehat{\mathbf{y}})
  =
  \arccos
  \frac{\inner{\mathbf{y}}{\widehat{\mathbf{y}}}}
  {\norm{\mathbf{y}}_2\norm{\widehat{\mathbf{y}}}_2+\epsilon},
\end{equation}
and then averaged over valid pixels. The small $\epsilon$ is used only to
avoid division by zero for empty or near-empty spectra. Before fitting, each
hyperspectral cube is divided by its maximum entry. All compared methods use
the same normalized cube and the same train/test masks.

\subsection{Hyperspectral Protocol}
\label{supp:hsi-protocol}

The CAVE multispectral image database \cite{cave_multispectral_database,
yasuma2010gapc} is used for the primary hyperspectral experiments. Reconstruction
uses all entries for fitting and evaluation. In the noise-robustness experiment,
Gaussian noise is added before fitting and metrics are computed against the
clean cube. Completion samples observation masks at the requested missing rates
and evaluates RMSE* and SAM* on the entries hidden during fitting. Tucker and
NTD-PL use identical Tucker ranks;
the default NTD-PL instance adds only the polynomial response coefficients and the
associated training schedule.
The reconstruction comparison in
Table~\ref{paper-tab:cave-main-baselines} uses Tucker ranks \((12,12,4)\),
\((24,24,4)\), and \((36,36,4)\), corresponding to approximately 13k, 27k,
and 42k parameters. At these budgets, the matched CP ranks are 12, 26, and 40,
and the TT ranks are \((1,5,5,1)\), \((1,7,7,1)\), and \((1,9,9,1)\),
respectively. At every budget, NTD-PL is refitted independently for
\(P=2,4,\) and \(6\).

The completion experiment uses Tucker rank \((33,33,4)\), \(P=4\), and 300
optimization iterations for both methods at missing rates \(0.1\), \(0.3\),
\(0.5\), and \(0.7\).

The noise-robustness evaluation uses all 15 CAVE scenes at their native
$512\times512\times31$ size with rank $(12,12,6)$ and \(P=4\). Gaussian noise is added
after global max normalization at SNR levels $40$, $30$, $20$, and $10$ dB;
the clean case uses the same protocol without added noise. The clean evaluation
tensor is shared by Tucker and NTD-PL for each scene, and both methods use 100
optimization iterations. Across the tested noise levels, NTD-PL reduces RMSE
by 6.16--6.20\% and SAM by 4.79--5.82\% relative to matched-rank Tucker.

\subsection{Mechanism Analysis Details}
\label{supp:mechanism-details}

The mechanism analysis in Figs.~\ref{paper-fig:mechanism-rank-inflation} and
\ref{paper-fig:mechanism-response} uses the \(512\times512\times31\) CAVE scenes at
rank $(12,12,4)$ and
sweeps the maximum power degree \(P\). The rank-inflation summary in
Table~\ref{tab:rank-inflation-mechanism} reports the degree-wise gain,
nonlinear-response rank, effective-rank increase, and tail capture.
Figure~\ref{paper-fig:error-regime} in the main paper visualizes the
observed-domain error regimes, and Table~\ref{tab:error-regime-mechanism}
reports the spectral breakdown.

For the rank-inflation calculation, we fit a separate Tucker baseline and
NTD-PL model for each scene and each \(P\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}\), using all entries of
the globally max-normalized cube. The Tucker rank is \((12,12,4)\), the random
seed is 0, and the NTD-PL runs use Tucker initialization, the power basis,
degree continuation, and 120 optimization iterations. If \(\widehat{\cS}\) is
the tensor reconstructed from the NTD-PL Tucker parameters and \(\widehat{\cY}\)
is its response-linked prediction, the nonlinear response component is
\[
 \widehat{\cR}_{\mathrm{nl}}
 =\widehat{\cY}-\widehat\beta_0\mathbf{1}
 -\widehat\beta_1\widehat{\cS}
 =\sum_{p=2}^{P}\widehat\beta_p\widehat{\cS}^{\od p}.
\]
For a tensor \(\mathcal{Z}\) and mode \(n\), define
\[
 r_{.999}(\mathcal{Z};n)=\min\left\{k:
 \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_j^2(\mathcal{Z}_{(n)})}
 {\sum_j\sigma_j^2(\mathcal{Z}_{(n)})}\geq 0.999\right\},
\]
where \(\mathcal{Z}_{(n)}\) is the mode-\(n\) unfolding. Fig.~\ref{paper-fig:mechanism-rank-inflation}
uses modes 1 and 2 and reports the median over the 30 scene--mode values for
each annotation. The effective-rank increase is computed as
\(r_{.999}(\widehat{\cY};n)-r_{.999}(\widehat{\cS};n)\).
The RMSE gain is computed per scene as
\[
 g_{\mathrm{RMSE}}=100\,
 \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{Tucker}}-
 \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{NTD\text{-}PL}}}
 {\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{Tucker}}},
\]
then averaged over scenes.
For the reported tail-capture correlation, tail energy is the fraction of
unfolding energy after the prescribed spatial Tucker rank 12. We divide the
prediction tail energy by the corresponding measured-tensor tail energy,
average modes 1 and 2 within each scene, and compute Spearman correlation with
RMSE gain over all 75 scene--degree pairs.

For the response-curve calculation, we refit NTD-PL independently for each
scene and each degree with the same rank, normalization, seed, and power
basis; these runs use 180 optimization iterations. For the fitted signal
values \(s_i\), we evaluate \(f_{\bbeta}(s_i)\) at 101 empirical quantiles from
the 1st to the 99th percentile. Each scene curve is endpoint-normalized as
\[
 x_i=\frac{s_i-s_{.01}}{s_{.99}-s_{.01}},\qquad
 y_i=\frac{f_{\bbeta}(s_i)-f_{\bbeta}(s_{.01})}
 {f_{\bbeta}(s_{.99})-f_{\bbeta}(s_{.01})}.
\]
The plotted dark curve is the pointwise mean over the 15 scene curves. To
compute the curvature label, let \(\Delta s_i=s_{i+1}-s_i\) and
\(d_i=(f_{\bbeta}(s_{i+1})-f_{\bbeta}(s_i))/\Delta s_i\). For each scene we use
\[
 \mathrm{Curv.}=
 \frac{1}{q_{.95}(|d|)}
 \frac{1}{m-2}\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}
 \left|\frac{d_{i+1}-d_i}
 {0.5(\Delta s_i+\Delta s_{i+1})}\right|,
\]
where \(m=101\) and \(q_{.95}(|d|)\) is the 95th percentile of the absolute
local slopes. The value shown in each panel is averaged over scenes. The gain
in each panel is relative to the mean RMSE at \(P=1\).

\subsection{Response Diagnostic Details}
\label{supp:diagnostic-details}

The diagnostic $\Dlink$ is computed from a fixed Tucker fit. First, a
matched-rank Tucker model is trained under the same mask and normalization as
the main experiment. Second, the Tucker reconstruction is held fixed and
the response coefficients are refit by the ridge update in
\eqref{paper-eq:link_refresh_solution}. The diagnostic is the residual-energy
ratio before and after this response-only fit, reported in dB as defined in
\eqref{paper-eq:dlink}. This
procedure is cheaper than joint NTD-PL training because it does not update the
Tucker core and factors through the nonlinear response.

For the CAVE diagnostic row, each scene is treated as one reconstruction task.
For the remaining rows, each image, object,
or video clip is treated as one independent tensor unit. In all cases,
\(\Dlink\) is computed before joint NTD-PL training. Units are sorted by
\(\Dlink\) within each dataset and divided into three groups of nearly equal
size; the table reports the mean diagnostic value and the eventual RMSE
reduction in each group.

\subsection{Compute Resources}
\label{supp:compute-resources}

The runs use a CPU-only NumPy/SciPy/TensorLy implementation under Python 3.13
and Windows 11. Timings were collected on 32 GB workstations with an Intel Core
i5-13400F or i7-14700F processor. Independent jobs were parallelized with
BLAS/OpenMP threads capped per worker; no GPU kernels were used. The
CAVE noise sweep used 12 workers on the i7-14700F system.

Controlled synthetic jobs took less than 1 s per configuration. For
CAVE reconstruction, Tucker took 10--17 s and NTD-PL 172--217 s per scene;
random completion took 168--258 s and 463--1019 s, respectively, per scene and
mask configuration. The CAVE response diagnostic took 7.0--9.6 s per scene.
With 6 workers, the non-HSI image/object batch took about 77 s and each
action-video batch took 5--6 min. The noise sweep required
9.7--32.6 s per Tucker fit and 61.0--167.7 s per NTD-PL fit; its 150 fits
completed in about 21 min. Peak memory was not instrumented, but all runs
completed within 32 GB RAM.
