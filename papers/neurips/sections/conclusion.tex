\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether a Tucker model can keep its chosen backbone ranks fixed while learning a
structured response that explains coherent nonlinear residuals left by matched-rank Tucker.
NTD-PL gives a positive but scoped answer. It places a polynomial link on a shared Tucker
latent tensor, so each higher-degree channel is not an independent high-rank correction but a
Veronese-lifted, parameter-tied rank lift of the same backbone. In this way, the model preserves
the core--factor structure and rank budget of Tucker while adding only a small number of scalar
link coefficients.

The analysis and optimizer are built around this shared-backbone view. The theory shows how
polynomial powers induce tied rank expansion, separates the resulting model class from
matched-rank Tucker, and provides approximation guarantees for nonlinear measurement
responses. The optimization procedure alternates a small Link Refresh ridge solve with
Linked-Backbone Descent and degree continuation, making the link a jointly learned part of
the representation rather than a post-hoc calibration layer.

The experiments identify the regime in which this additional structure is useful. Controlled
residual experiments show that NTD-PL stays close to Tucker in linear or nearly linear settings
and gains when the residual is nonlinear and expressible by the active polynomial degrees.
CAVE reconstruction and completion show that the same fixed-backbone enhancement improves
both representation and held-out prediction, while the rank-lift ablation shows that the gain is
not merely a parameter-count effect. Finally, the Link Refresh diagnostic predicts where full
NTD-PL helps across HSI and non-HSI tensor units. These results position NTD-PL not as a
universal nonlinear tensor decoder, but as a compact Tucker enhancement for tensors whose
matched-rank residuals are coherent with a shared scalar response. Future work may extend this
idea to adaptive links, mode- or region-dependent responses, and scalable sparse implementations.
