\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

This section summarizes the structural, optimization, and computational
properties of dictionary-linked Tucker models. The rank results are stated for
the power dictionary, where the rank expansion has an explicit algebraic form.
The optimization results apply to any differentiable finite dictionary
whose response is linear in the response coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Tied Tucker rank expansion]
\label{prop:rank_expansion}
Let $\cS$ have Tucker rank at most $(R_1,\ldots,R_N)$. For every $p \ge 1$,
$\cS^{\od p}$ admits a Tucker representation with mode ranks at most
\begin{equation}
  \left(
  \binom{R_1+p-1}{p},\ldots,\binom{R_N+p-1}{p}
  \right).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Thus each polynomial channel can be represented as an ordinary Tucker tensor
with lifted factors, but those lifted factors remain tied to the same original
factors. This is the structural difference between the proposed model and an
untied higher-rank correction.

\begin{corollary}[Parameter economy of tied polynomial channels]
\label{cor:tied_lift_parameters}
Let $D_n(p)=\binom{R_n+p-1}{p}$. A Tucker model that explicitly represents the
degree-$p$ channel in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_expansion} may require
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{lift}}(p)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}D_n(p)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_nD_n(p)
\end{equation}
parameters for that channel alone. The power-dictionary linked model represents all
degrees $0,\ldots,Q$ with the original Tucker parameters plus $Q+1$ response
coefficients. Hence the extra parameter cost of the linked model is independent
of the ambient dimensions $\{I_n\}$ and the lifted ranks $\{D_n(p)\}$.
\end{corollary}

This corollary does not imply that an explicit higher-rank Tucker model is a
poor model; it explains why the linked model is economical when the high-rank
structure is specifically generated by a shared entrywise response.

\begin{theorem}[Separation from matched-rank Tucker]
\label{thm:tucker_separation}
Let \(N\ge 3\), \(R\ge 2\), and \(p\ge 2\). Define
\(D=\binom{R+p-1}{p}\) and suppose \(I_n\ge D\) for every mode. Then there
exists an order-\(N\) tensor representable by a degree-\(p\)
polynomial-linked Tucker model with Tucker rank \((R,\ldots,R)\), but whose
exact standard Tucker rank is \((D,\ldots,D)\).
\end{theorem}

The theorem gives a strict algebraic separation: the power response does more than
post-calibrate an already fitted tensor. It can generate tensors whose
ordinary multilinear ranks match the Veronese-lifted ranks in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_expansion}, while the trainable Tucker signal remains at
rank \((R,\ldots,R)\). This is an existence result for response-generated
high-rank structure, not a claim that the linked model spans all tensors of the
larger Tucker rank.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial response generation]
\label{thm:poly_generation}
If $\cY=g(\cS^\star)$, where $\cS^\star$ has Tucker rank at most
$(R_1,\ldots,R_N)$ and $g$ is a polynomial of degree at most $P$, then a
degree-$P$ polynomial-linked Tucker model with the same Tucker rank can
represent $\cY$ exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Continuous response approximation]
\label{prop:response_approximation}
Let $\cS^\star$ have Tucker rank at most $(R_1,\ldots,R_N)$ and entries in a
compact interval $[a,b]$. If the measurement response $f$ is continuous on
$[a,b]$, then for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists a polynomial degree $P$ and
coefficients $\bbeta$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \norm{f(\cS^\star)-f_{\bbeta}(\cS^\star)}_F
  \le
  \epsilon \sqrt{M},
  \qquad
  M=\prod_{n=1}^{N}I_n .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Thus the power response is not restricted to exactly polynomial sensors. On a
bounded signal range, it can approximate any smooth or continuous entrywise
response to the desired uniform accuracy, while keeping the Tucker rank of the
underlying signal fixed. The degree required for a given accuracy depends on the regularity of
the response and the width of the signal range. This approximation result is
the reason the power dictionary is used as the default response basis in the
experiments.

\begin{remark}[Gauge and identifiability]
\label{rem:gauge}
As in ordinary Tucker models, the factorization of $\cS$ is not unique: mode
factors can be rescaled or transformed with an inverse transformation absorbed
by the core. The response function adds another scale interaction because
changing the scale of $\cS$ changes the natural scale of the response coefficients.
The estimator therefore fixes a numerical gauge by normalizing factor columns
and absorbing inverse scales into the core. This does not make the Tucker
factors identifiable in a strict statistical sense, but it keeps the
represented signal and response numerically well conditioned and makes
the objective in Section~\ref{sec:estimation} a stable parameterization of the
same fitted measurement tensor.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Descent and Stationarity}

We analyze the fixed-dictionary objective $\mathcal{F}_Q$ in
\eqref{eq:estimation_objective}. For nested dictionaries, the continuation
schedule can be viewed as a sequence of such fixed-dictionary problems, each
initialized from the previous dictionary size.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:optimization}
For a fixed active dictionary size $Q+1$, the iterates remain in a bounded normalized
parameter set. The regularization weights are nonnegative and
$\lambda_\beta>0$. The Tucker step either uses a line search or an equivalent
descent rule such that, for constants $c>0$ and $b>0$,
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^k)-\mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^{k+1})
  &\ge c\norm{\theta^{k+1}-\theta^k}_2^2,
  \label{eq:sufficient_decrease}\\
  \mathrm{dist}\!\left(0,\partial \mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^{k+1})\right)
  &\le b\norm{\theta^{k+1}-\theta^k}_2 .
  \label{eq:relative_error}
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

The boundedness condition is enforced in practice by Tucker gauge
normalization and regularization. The two descent conditions are standard for
block coordinate and line-search gradient schemes on smooth objectives.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone objective decrease]
\label{prop:monotone_descent}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:optimization}, every fixed-dictionary outer iteration of
the alternating estimator satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^{k+1})\le \mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^k).
\end{equation}
Moreover, if \eqref{eq:sufficient_decrease} holds for the full outer update,
then
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
  \norm{\theta^{k+1}-\theta^k}_2^2
  <\infty .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence to a stationary point]
\label{thm:stationary_convergence}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:optimization} holds for a fixed dictionary size
$Q+1$. Then
the sequence generated by the alternating estimator has finite length and
converges to a critical point $\theta^\star$ of $\mathcal{F}_Q$:
\begin{equation}
  0\in \partial \mathcal{F}_Q(\theta^\star).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The theorem does not claim global optimality; the problem remains nonconvex
because of the Tucker factorization. Its role is to rule out pathological cycling
for the proposed alternating estimator and to place the algorithm on the same
theoretical footing as standard nonconvex block-descent methods
\cite{Attouch2013Convergence,Bolte2014PALM}.

\subsection{Diagnostic Interpretation}

The diagnostic $\Dlink$ in \eqref{eq:dlink} can be interpreted as a projection
test for residual structure that depends on the current Tucker estimate. Let $\bPhi_{\Omega,Q}$ be the response
feature matrix built from a frozen Tucker reconstruction $\cS_T$ and let
$\mathbf{P}_Q$ denote the orthogonal projector onto its column space. When
$\lambda_\beta=0$, the refreshed-response residual satisfies
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{link}}
  =
  \norm{(\bI-\mathbf{P}_Q)y_\Omega}_2^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\Dlink$ is the fraction of identity-response residual energy removed
by projecting the observations onto the response-feature subspace generated
by the frozen Tucker fit. With ridge regularization, the same interpretation
holds with $\mathbf{P}_Q$ replaced by the corresponding shrinkage operator. Large
$\Dlink$ therefore means that the residual is aligned with scalar functions of
the current Tucker estimate, whereas small $\Dlink$ suggests that the residual
is better addressed by increasing multilinear rank, changing the tensor prior,
or revisiting the observation model.

\subsection{Masked Generalization}

The masked objective also has the usual sampling interpretation. Let
\(\Theta\subset\R^d\) be a bounded parameter set for a degree-\(P\)
polynomial-linked Tucker model, where
\begin{equation}
  d=
  \prod_{n=1}^{N}R_n
  +
  \sum_{n=1}^{N}I_nR_n
  +
  (P+1).
\end{equation}
For \(\theta\in\Theta\), define the full-entry risk
\(\mathcal{L}(\theta)=M^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{i}}\ell_{\mathbf{i}}(\theta)\),
\(M=\prod_n I_n\), and the masked empirical risk
\(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_m(\theta)=m^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{m}
\ell_{\mathbf{i}_t}(\theta)\), where the entries
\(\mathbf{i}_t\) are sampled uniformly.

\begin{theorem}[Masked-entry uniform convergence]
\label{thm:masked_generalization}
Assume \(0\le \ell_{\mathbf{i}}(\theta)\le B_\ell\) and
\(\ell_{\mathbf{i}}(\theta)\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz in \(\theta\), uniformly over
\(\mathbf{i}\). If \(\Theta\) lies in a Euclidean ball of radius \(B_\Theta\),
then, with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
  \left|
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)-\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_m(\theta)
  \right|
  \le
  2G\varepsilon
  +
  B_\ell
  \sqrt{
  \frac{
  d\log(3B_\Theta/\varepsilon)+\log(2/\delta)}
  {2m}}
\end{equation}
for every \(0<\varepsilon\le B_\Theta\).
\end{theorem}

This bound is not intended as a sharp sample-complexity result. Its role is to
make clear which complexity enters the masked recovery problem: the covering
dimension is the tied dictionary-linked parameter count, rather than the
parameter count of an untied Veronese-rank Tucker expansion.

\subsection{Computational Scaling}

Let $M=\prod_{n=1}^{N}I_n$, $R_\Pi=\prod_{n=1}^{N}R_n$, and
$d_{\mathrm{Tucker}}=R_\Pi+\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_nR_n$. Storing the dictionary-linked
model requires
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{NTD}}=d_{\mathrm{Tucker}}+(Q+1)
\end{equation}
parameters. A rank-expanded Tucker model that explicitly matches the degree-$Q$
Veronese lift may require mode ranks
$D_n=\binom{R_n+Q-1}{Q}$ for the degree-$Q$ channel, with core size
$\prod_n D_n$ and factor size $\sum_n I_nD_n$. The proposed model keeps the
storage cost tied to the original Tucker core and factors and avoids explicit storage of the
lifted tensor.

For one outer iteration, Response Refresh forms
$\bPhi_{\Omega,Q}^{T}\bPhi_{\Omega,Q}$ and solves a small system, costing
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{O}\!\left(mQ^2+Q^3\right).
\end{equation}
The dominant Tucker cost is evaluating the Tucker reconstruction and reverse
contractions on the observed set or on the full tensor, depending on the
implementation. Dense reconstruction scales as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{O}\!\left(
  M R_\Pi + \sum_{n=1}^{N} I_n R_n R_\Pi/R_n
  \right)
\end{equation}
up to constants determined by contraction order, followed by an additional
$\mathcal{O}(MQ)$ cost to evaluate the entrywise response for dense dictionaries. In sparse masked
settings, the same operations can be restricted to observed entries, replacing
$M$ by $m$ in the response and residual computations. Since $Q$ is small, the
response-update cost is typically negligible compared with the Tucker contractions.
