\begin{abstract}
Many multidimensional signals admit compact low-rank tensor representations,
but nonlinear measurements can make a low-rank signal appear to have a higher
Tucker rank after observation. We first introduce a Tucker polynomial model,
which extends the linear map from a Tucker core to the data tensor with
higher-order interactions. Its general form learns new mode maps for each
polynomial order. Sharing the same Tucker factors across orders yields NTD-PL,
a compact form that is exactly a low-rank Tucker tensor followed by an
entrywise polynomial response. The same estimator also supports other finite
response bases. We analyze the resulting rank inflation, parameter cost,
approximation of continuous responses, stationarity under standard descent
conditions, and computational scaling. We also introduce a low-cost diagnostic that tests
how much of a Tucker residual can be removed by refitting only the response.
Experiments on controlled data, CAVE hyperspectral reconstruction and
completion, noisy measurements, and several non-hyperspectral datasets show
consistent improvements over matched-rank Tucker and competitive low-rank
tensor baselines. Larger gains occur when the learned response increases the
effective rank of the fitted tensor and when a response-only refit removes more
of the Tucker residual.
\end{abstract}
