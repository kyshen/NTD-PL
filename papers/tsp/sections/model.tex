\section{Dictionary-Linked Tucker Representation}
\label{sec:model}

We first describe the nonlinear measurement model and then introduce the
dictionary-linked Tucker representation used for recovery. The goal is not to
define a new tensor task, but to make explicit a common source of model
mismatch: the tensor that is compact before measurement may be observed
after a nonlinear response.

\subsection{Nonlinear Low-Rank Signal Model}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\cY \in \R^{I_1 \times \cdots \times I_N}$ denote the measured tensor and
let $\Omega \in \{0,1\}^{I_1 \times \cdots \times I_N}$ be the observation
mask. We assume that the measured entries are generated from an underlying
signal $\cS$ through an entrywise response,
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathbf{i}} = f(S_{\mathbf{i}}) + \varepsilon_{\mathbf{i}},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{i}=(i_1,\ldots,i_N),
  \label{eq:measurement_model}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\mathbf{i}}$ denotes measurement noise. The underlying
signal is represented by a Tucker model,
\begin{equation}
  \cS =
  \tucker{\cX}{\bA^{(1)},\ldots,\bA^{(N)}} ,
  \label{eq:latent_tucker}
\end{equation}
where $\cX \in \R^{R_1 \times \cdots \times R_N}$ is the core and
$\bA^{(n)} \in \R^{I_n \times R_n}$ are mode factors.

This model separates two notions of complexity. The underlying signal is controlled
by the Tucker rank $(R_1,\ldots,R_N)$, while the measured tensor may have larger
effective multilinear rank because of the response $f$. The recovery problem is
therefore to estimate the low-rank Tucker structure and the response from the
observed entries of $\cY$.

\subsection{Scalar Link Dictionaries}

We approximate the unknown response in \eqref{eq:measurement_model} by a
finite scalar dictionary,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\cY}
  =
  f_{\bbeta}(\cS)
  =
  \sum_{q=0}^{Q} \beta_q \psi_q(\cS),
  \label{eq:dictionary_linked_tucker}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_q(\cS)$ applies $\psi_q$ entrywise. Standard Tucker is recovered
whenever the dictionary contains the identity channel and the fitted response is
$f_{\bbeta}(s)=s$. The default choice is the power dictionary
$\psi_q(s)=s^q$, which gives the polynomial-linked Tucker model
\begin{equation}
  f_{\bbeta}(\cS)
  =
  \sum_{p=0}^{P} \beta_p \cS^{\od p}.
  \label{eq:polynomial_linked_tucker}
\end{equation}
For $p\ge 2$, the degree-$p$ channel $\cS^{\od p}$ uses products of entries
from the same Tucker signal. Thus the model can increase effective
multilinear rank without adding an independent high-rank tensor block.
Appendix~\ref{app:pfold_tucker_motivation} gives the explicit
\(p\)-fold Tucker construction from which this tied channel is obtained.

The same estimator can also support non-power dictionaries that remain linear
in the coefficients $\bbeta$, for example conditioned polynomial bases, radial
basis functions, or splines. These choices change the entrywise response family
but not the Tucker core and factors, the masked objective, or the response-refresh
subproblem. Unless otherwise stated, the remainder of the paper uses the power
dictionary.

\subsection{Masked Recovery Objective}

The same formulation covers full reconstruction and completion. We estimate
the core, factors, and response coefficients by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\cX,\{\bA^{(n)}\},\bbeta}
  \frac{1}{2|\Omega|}
  \norm{\Omega \od \left(\cY - f_{\bbeta}(\cS)\right)}_F^2
  +
  \mathcal{R}(\cX,\{\bA^{(n)}\},\bbeta),
  \label{eq:masked_objective_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}$ contains regularization terms for the Tucker parameters and
the response coefficients. When $\Omega$ is the all-one tensor, \eqref{eq:masked_objective_model}
gives full reconstruction. When $\Omega$ selects a subset of entries, the loss
is evaluated only on observed measurements and prediction is assessed on the
held-out entries.
