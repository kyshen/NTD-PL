\section{Estimation Algorithm and Response Diagnostic}
\label{sec:estimation}

We estimate the shared-factor model in
\eqref{eq:finite_response_tucker}. The general Tucker polynomial in
\eqref{eq:tucker_polynomial} motivates the construction, but its separate
high-order mode maps are not part of the estimator.

Let $m=|\Omega|$ and let
$\theta=(\cX,\{\bA^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{N},\bbeta)$ collect all trainable
parameters. For an active response basis with $J+1$ functions, we minimize
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{F}_J(\theta)
  =
  \frac{1}{2|\Omega|}
  \norm{\Omega \od \left(\cY - f_{\bbeta}(\cS)\right)}_F^2
  +
  \frac{\lambda_X}{2}\norm{\cX}_F^2 \\
  \quad
  +
  \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_n}{2}\norm{\bA^{(n)}}_F^2
  +
  \frac{\lambda_\beta}{2}\norm{\bbeta}_2^2,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:estimation_objective}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\bbeta}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\beta_j\psi_j(s)$ and
$\{\psi_j\}_{j=0}^{J}$ is the selected response basis. For the power basis,
$J=P$ and its $p$th function is $\psi_p(s)=s^p$, which gives
\eqref{eq:polynomial_linked_tucker}; in
implementation, the same polynomial space may be represented by centered,
Chebyshev, or otherwise conditioned bases. Non-polynomial choices such as RBF
or spline bases use the same objective as long as the response remains linear
in $\bbeta$. The experiments use the monomial power basis. The
estimation alternates between an exact small response-coefficient update and a
descent step for the Tucker core and factors.

\subsection{Response Refresh as a Ridge Subproblem}

Fix the current Tucker signal $\cS$ and list the observed entries as
$y_\Omega\in\R^m$ and $s_\Omega\in\R^m$. Define the feature matrix
$\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS)\in\R^{m\times(J+1)}$ by
\begin{equation}
  [\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS)]_{t,j}=\psi_j([s_\Omega]_t).
\end{equation}
Here $t=1,\ldots,m$ indexes the observed entries and
$j=0,\ldots,J$ indexes the basis functions.
The response update is the ridge regression
\begin{equation}
  \bbeta^+
  =
  \argmin_{\bbeta\in\R^{J+1}}
  \frac{1}{2m}
  \norm{y_\Omega-\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS)\bbeta}_2^2
  +
  \frac{\lambda_\beta}{2}\norm{\bbeta}_2^2,
  \label{eq:link_refresh_problem}
\end{equation}
with closed-form solution
\begin{equation}
  \bbeta^+
  =
  \left(
  \frac{1}{m}\bPhi_{\Omega,J}^{T}\bPhi_{\Omega,J}
  +\lambda_\beta \bI
  \right)^{-1}
  \frac{1}{m}\bPhi_{\Omega,J}^{T}y_\Omega.
  \label{eq:link_refresh_solution}
\end{equation}
This step solves only a $(J+1)\times(J+1)$ system. It is therefore cheap
relative to reconstructing or differentiating through the Tucker tensor, and
because it solves the $\bbeta$-subproblem exactly, it never increases
$\mathcal{F}_J$ for the current Tucker signal.

\subsection{Descent for the Tucker Parameters}

With $\bbeta$ fixed, the residual is backpropagated through the entrywise response
and then through the Tucker map. The gradient with respect to the Tucker signal
is
\begin{equation}
  \bG_{\cS}
  =
  \frac{1}{m}
  \Omega\od
  \left(f_{\bbeta}(\cS)-\cY\right)
  \od f_{\bbeta}'(\cS).
  \label{eq:latent_gradient}
\end{equation}
The Tucker gradients are standard reverse contractions:
\begin{align}
  \nabla_{\cX}\mathcal{F}_J
  &=
  \bG_{\cS}
  \times_1 (\bA^{(1)})^T
  \cdots
  \times_N (\bA^{(N)})^T
  +\lambda_X\cX, \\
  \nabla_{\bA^{(n)}}\mathcal{F}_J
  &=
  [\bG_{\cS}]_{(n)}
  \bZ_n^T
  +
  \lambda_n\bA^{(n)},
  \label{eq:factor_gradient}
\end{align}
where
$\bZ_n=(\bA^{(N)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bA^{(n+1)}\otimes
\bA^{(n-1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bA^{(1)})\cX_{(n)}^T$.
For the convergence result in Section~\ref{sec:theory}, the Tucker update is
taken to satisfy a sufficient decrease condition, as with a line-search or
suitable block-coordinate step. The experiments use Adam as a practical
first-order solver.

\subsection{Response-Basis Continuation and Normalization}

Large or high-order response bases can amplify Tucker scale ambiguity. For
nested bases, such as the power or Chebyshev bases, we use continuation:
training starts with the Tucker-compatible identity response and activates
additional basis functions gradually. When a new function is introduced, its coefficient
is initialized at zero, so the prediction is unchanged at the moment of
activation. Non-nested bases such as RBF and spline bases are trained with all
selected basis functions from the start. After Tucker updates, factor
columns are rescaled and the inverse scales are absorbed into the core. This
fixes a numerical gauge without changing the represented signal $\cS$ or the
data-fitting term. It does not make the Tucker factors identifiable in a strict
statistical sense; it only keeps the Tucker parameterization and response
coefficients on stable numerical scales during training.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Alternating recovery for NTD-PL and other finite response bases.}
  \label{alg:alternating_recovery}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE measured tensor $\cY$, mask $\Omega$, Tucker ranks
    $(R_1,\ldots,R_N)$, response basis $\{\psi_j\}$, regularization
    weights, continuation schedule
    \STATE Initialize $\cX$ and $\{\bA^{(n)}\}$ from a Tucker fit or a random
    Tucker factorization.
    \STATE Set the active basis to the identity response and initialize
    $\bbeta=(0,1,0,\ldots,0)$.
    \FOR{outer iterations $k=0,1,\ldots$}
      \STATE Form the current Tucker signal
      $\cS=\tucker{\cX}{\bA^{(1)},\ldots,\bA^{(N)}}$.
      \STATE Build $\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS)$ on the observed entries.
      \STATE Refresh $\bbeta$ by the ridge solution in
      \eqref{eq:link_refresh_solution}.
      \STATE Compute $\bG_{\cS}$ from \eqref{eq:latent_gradient}.
      \STATE Update $\cX$ and $\{\bA^{(n)}\}$ by a descent step using
      \eqref{eq:latent_gradient}--\eqref{eq:factor_gradient}.
      \STATE Normalize factor columns and absorb inverse scales into $\cX$.
      \IF{the continuation schedule activates a new basis function}
        \STATE Add the new response coefficient and initialize it at zero.
      \ENDIF
      \IF{the stopping criterion is met}
        \STATE Stop.
      \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \ENSURE fitted Tucker signal $\cS$, response coefficients $\bbeta$, and
    prediction $f_{\bbeta}(\cS)$
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:alternating_recovery} treats the response as part of the
fitted representation: the Tucker update is computed through the current
nonlinear measurement map, rather than by post-processing a fixed Tucker fit.

\subsection{Response Diagnostic}

The diagnostic measures how much the residual of a linear Tucker fit decreases
when only the entrywise response is refit. Let $\cS_T$ be a fitted Tucker
reconstruction, and let $\bbeta_T=(0,1,0,\ldots,0)$ denote the identity response
in the degree-$J$ basis. We first compute
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\bbeta}_{\mathrm{resp}}
  =\argmin_{\bbeta}\;
  \norm{y_\Omega-\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS_T)\bbeta}_2^2
  +m\lambda_\beta\norm{\bbeta}_2^2.
  \label{eq:diagnostic-response-fit}
\end{align}
The residual energies before and after this response-only fit are
\begin{align}
  R_T
  &=\norm{y_\Omega-\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS_T)\bbeta_T}_2^2, \\
  R_{\mathrm{resp}}
  &=\norm{y_\Omega-\bPhi_{\Omega,J}(\cS_T)
  \widehat{\bbeta}_{\mathrm{resp}}}_2^2.
\end{align}
We report their ratio on a decibel scale:
\begin{align}
  \Dlink
  &=
  10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{R_T}{R_{\mathrm{resp}}}\right)\ \mathrm{dB}.
  \label{eq:dlink}
\end{align}
A large positive $\Dlink$ means that refitting only the response coefficients
substantially reduces the residual without changing the Tucker fit. We use this
score as a model-selection and interpretation tool: a small or nonpositive score
suggests that increasing multilinear rank or changing the tensor prior may be
more appropriate.
