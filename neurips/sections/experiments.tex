\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments test three mechanism questions. First, under controlled
nonlinear residuals, does NTD-PL behave like a parameter-tied rank lift on top
of a fixed Tucker backbone? Second, on real tensors, does the polynomial link
improve matched-rank Tucker in full reconstruction and held-out completion?
Third, across broader data domains, which tensor units exhibit
nonlinear low-rank structure, and when does that structure make NTD-PL
meaningfully beneficial beyond a linear Tucker fit? Unless stated
otherwise, real tensors are globally max-normalized and used without spatial
downsampling or cropping. RMSE and SAM are used for reconstruction; RMSE$^\ast$
and SAM$^\ast$ are computed only on held-out missing entries for completion.
NTD-PL is compared mainly against Tucker at the same multilinear rank, except
in the explicit rank-lift ablation. For the cross-domain question, we also use
the diagnostic score \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\), defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:external-hsi-validity}.

\subsection{Controlled Nonlinear Residuals}
\label{subsec:controlled-experiments}

These synthetic experiments are mechanism checks rather than generic
benchmarks. We generate measured tensors as
\[
Y_\alpha=\sqrt{1-\alpha}S^\star+\sqrt{\alpha}\widetilde{R}^\star,
\]
where \(S^\star\) is a low-rank Tucker tensor and \(\widetilde{R}^\star\) is
the normalized component of a scalar nonlinear response orthogonal to
\(S^\star\). Thus \(\alpha\) controls nonlinear residual energy, not just the
raw amplitude of a nonlinearity. Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-alpha-grid} shows
the expected transition: NTD-PL is Tucker-like when the residual is nearly
linear and separates as nonlinear residual energy increases. Tucker can lag
behind CP, but NTD-PL then pulls ahead systematically.
Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-pmax-strip} shows the complementary degree effect:
gains emerge when the active polynomial degree can match or approximate the
response complexity. Degree continuation and coefficient-contribution details
are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:controlled-synthetic}, where Table~\ref{tab:linear-consistency-controlled}
shows the linear consistency negative control. Taken together, these checks
indicate that NTD-PL is not a blanket improvement rule; its gain depends on
the data's nonlinear structure.

\subsection{Tucker Enhancement on Real Hyperspectral Tensors}
\label{subsec:lowrank-completion}

This section tests whether the same fixed-rank enhancement appears on real
hyperspectral tensors, both when the tensor is fully measured and when
evaluation is performed on held-out missing entries.
We use CAVE dataset because it offers multiple real scenes with rich spectral and
spatial structure, making it a reasonable mechanism check rather than a
toy-sized tensor.

\paragraph{Representation enhancement.}
Full reconstruction is a representation-capacity check, not a generalization
test. Figure~\ref{fig:cave-reconstruction-capacity} shows that NTD-PL improves
RMSE and SAM on CAVE at matched Tucker backbone rank and similar parameter
budget. The gap shrinks as Tucker rank increases, consistent with a fixed-rank
enhancement: ordinary multilinear capacity already explains more of the
tensor. Table~\ref{tab:rank-lift-tucker} gives the complementary rank-lift
ablation. Tucker can match the NTD-PL RMSE only after spending substantially
more ordinary rank and parameters, supporting the parameter-tied rank-lift
interpretation. Even when RMSE is close, NTD-PL usually retains a clearer SAM
advantage.

\paragraph{Held-out enhancement.}
Held-out completion is the stronger evidence because it tests whether the
learned geometry transfers to unseen entries. Table~
\ref{tab:lowrank-completion-main} reports CAVE completion under random masks,
with RMSE$^\ast$ and SAM$^\ast$ computed only on missing entries. At the same
rank budget, NTD-PL gives consistent held-out RMSE$^\ast$ gains and usually
improves SAM$^\ast$ as well. The paired sign and Wilcoxon tests in
Appendix~\ref{app:single-rank-completion} show the same scene-level pattern.

\subsection{Diagnosing Nonlinear Low-Rank Structure Across Data}
\label{subsec:external-hsi-validity}

NTD-PL is meant to be diagnosable rather than uniformly beneficial. Its gain
should appear when matched-rank Tucker leaves a coherent residual that a scalar
link can reduce. We measure this with \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\), a cheap
frozen-backbone Link Refresh probe: after fitting Tucker, freeze the backbone,
run one scalar Link Refresh ridge solve, and measure residual-energy reduction
on the relevant evaluation samples. Larger \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) indicates
more link-like residual structure:
\[
D_{\mathrm{link}}
=
10\log_{10}
\frac{
  \|Y_{\mathrm{eval}}-\widehat{Y}_{T}\|_F^2
}{
  \|Y_{\mathrm{eval}}-\widehat{Y}_{\mathrm{link}}\|_F^2
},
\]
where \(\widehat{Y}_{T}\) is the Tucker output and
\(\widehat{Y}_{\mathrm{link}}\) is the output after the one-step refresh.

For HSI, we use natural scene-level tensor units rather than artificial
patches. CAVE already provides multiple scene tensors, so
Figure~\ref{fig:cave-link-yield} evaluates whether higher
\(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) predicts larger held-out RMSE$^\ast$ gains.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/cave_link_yield_scatter.pdf}
  \caption{CAVE completion diagnostic. Scene-level \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) from
  frozen-backbone Link Refresh predicts larger held-out RMSE$^\ast$ gains at
  missing rates 0.3 and 0.5; panels annotate Spearman correlations.}
  \label{fig:cave-link-yield}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:cave-link-yield} first validates the diagnostic on CAVE
completion: scenes with higher \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) receive larger held-out
RMSE$^\ast$ gains. We then apply the same per-unit protocol beyond HSI. Each
natural image is one tensor unit; each object or instance forms an object-view
tensor; each KTH segment or UCF101 clip is an action-video tensor. We do not
concatenate unrelated images, objects, or clips, because \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\)
tests whether one scalar link is coherent within a unit.

Table~\ref{tab:cross-domain-link-diagnostic} summarizes the non-HSI diagnosis
on full reconstruction units. Natural images show weaker diagnostic yield,
especially CIFAR-10, whose median \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) is close to zero and
whose low-yield tercile has almost no gain. Object-view tensors show clearer
yield: COIL-100 and smallNORB have larger median \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\),
stronger rank-matched gains, and larger high-yield tercile improvements.
Action-video clips are intermediate; KTH-Action is stronger than UCF101, but
both retain positive diagnostic correlation and larger high-yield gains. These
results support the scoped claim: NTD-PL helps when the Tucker residual
contains link-like structure, not uniformly across all visual tensors.
Representative units selected only by \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:nonhsi-link-diagnostics}.
