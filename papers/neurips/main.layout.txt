                                                         Abstract

 1             Classical Tucker models explain multiway data through multilinear low-rank struc-
 2             ture, but many measurement processes introduce nonlinear entry-level effects such
 3             as saturation, response curves, and material-dependent nonlinearities. We study
 4             a focused question: can one add nonlinear observation capacity to Tucker while
 5             preserving its parameter-efficient low-rank backbone?
 6             We propose Nonlinear Tucker Decomposition with Polynomial Links (NTD-PL),
 7             which applies a finite polynomial link to a shared Tucker latent tensor. The model
 8             contains Tucker as a linear special case, acts as a compressed surrogate for high-
 9             order Tucker interactions, and admits masked learning through alternating gradient
10             updates and a small ridge-regression subproblem for the link coefficients. We give
11             approximation and separation results, together with a masked-entry generalization
12             bound. Experiments on controlled tensors, CAVE hyperspectral completion and
13             reconstruction, and external HSI scenes show that NTD-PL improves matched-rank
14             Tucker when a shared nonlinear residual is present, that the effect is not explained
15             by modest rank increases or post-hoc scalar calibration, and that boundary cases
16             arise when Tucker already leaves little coherent residual for the link to capture.



17   1    Introduction

18   Tensor decompositions provide compact models for multiway data in scientific sensing, imaging,
19   and signal processing [Kolda and Bader, 2009, Sidiropoulos et al., 2017, Liu et al., 2013]. Tucker
20   decomposition is especially useful because it separates a small core from mode-wise factors, but
21   its observation model is linear in the reconstructed latent entry. This is a strong assumption in
22   measurement pipelines where latent signals may pass through saturation, radiometric response curves,
23   material-dependent nonlinearities, or other entry-level generation effects. After such a response, an
24   intrinsically low-rank latent tensor can appear substantially higher-rank to a linear Tucker model.
25   One way to absorb this mismatch is to increase Tucker rank, but doing so expands the core and factor
26   dimensions everywhere in the model. Another is to calibrate the fitted Tucker output post hoc, but
27   that leaves the learned latent geometry fixed. We ask a narrower question: can a small nonlinear
28   observation model, learned jointly with the Tucker factors, make a tight multilinear rank budget more
29   effective? This paper focuses on the case where the residual is well described by a shared entry-level
30   response. The goal is therefore not to replace rank selection or general nonlinear tensor decoders, but
31   to isolate a simple mechanism by which nonlinear observation effects can be represented inside a
32   Tucker backbone.
33   We instantiate this idea with a polynomial link over a multilinear low-rank latent tensor. Given a

40   Contributions.    We make four contributions.

41   1. We introduce NTD-PL, a Tucker model with a jointly learned polynomial observation link, with
42      standard Tucker recovered as the degree-one special case.
43   2. We show that polynomial links act as compressed high-order Tucker interactions, yielding approx-
44      imation and separation results for nonlinear low-rank observations.
45   3. We give a masked learning objective, a practical alternating algorithm with degree continuation,
46      and a masked-entry generalization bound.
47   4. We empirically test the mechanism rather than a broad benchmark claim: controlled tensors isolate
48      when the link should help, held-out CAVE completion tests low-rank generalization, reconstruction
49      checks separate the effect from rank-lift and post-hoc calibration, and external HSI scenes show
50      both transfer and boundary cases.

51   2   Related Work
52   Low-rank tensor models. Classical tensor decompositions such as CP, Tucker, tensor train, and
53   tensor ring provide compact representations for multiway data [Kolda and Bader, 2009, De Lathauwer
54   et al., 2000, Oseledets, 2011, Zhao et al., 2016]. Tucker is the closest baseline for our work
55   because its core tensor and mode factors give a flexible multilinear backbone. Most methods in this
56   family improve the representation by changing ranks, factor structures, regularizers, or optimization
57   procedures. NTD-PL keeps the same Tucker parameterization and instead changes the observation
58   map from the latent tensor to the measured entries.

59   Nonlinear tensor factorization. Prior work has introduced nonlinearity through generalized like-
60   lihoods, kernels, Gaussian processes, neural decoders, and nonlinear tensor network architectures
61   [Hong et al., 2020, Xu et al., 2011, Zhe et al., 2016, Pan et al., 2020, Saragadam et al., 2024, Varol-
62   gunes et al., 2023]. These approaches are often more expressive than polynomial links, but they can
63   trade off structural transparency, optimization simplicity, or explicit finite-dimensional analysis. Our
64   goal is complementary. We use a minimal shared observation link so that the nonlinear effect can still
65   be analyzed as a structured extension of Tucker rather than as an unconstrained decoder.

66   Polynomial feature lifts and high-order interactions. Polynomial expansions and Volterra-style
67   models are classical tools for representing nonlinear systems. In tensor settings, high-order inter-
68   actions can be represented explicitly but often incur rapidly growing parameter and contraction
69   costs. Polynomial matrix and tensor factorization methods use feature expansions to increase ex-
70   pressivity, but they typically introduce larger lifted spaces, independent high-order parameters, or
71   model-specific factor structures. NTD-PL can be viewed as a shared-backbone compression of
72   high-order Tucker interactions: the lifted features are induced by powers of one learned latent tensor,
73   not by an independent high-order tensor parameter.

74   Rank lifting and post-hoc calibration. Two natural alternatives explain why the empirical com-
75   parisons are organized as mechanism checks. Rank lifting gives Tucker more ordinary multilinear
76   capacity and can absorb part of the nonlinear residual, but it enlarges the core and factors throughout
77   the model. Post-hoc scalar calibration is cheaper, but it remaps a Tucker reconstruction after the
78   latent geometry has already been learned. NTD-PL sits between these alternatives: it adds only a
79   small shared link, but learns that link jointly with the Tucker factors.

113   Practical scaling. Like Tucker, NTD-PL has scale indeterminacies between the core and mode
114   factors. Polynomial powers make this numerically more sensitive: if the scale of S drifts, different
115   powers change at different rates and the link coefficients become poorly conditioned. In the imple-
116   mentation we initialize from Tucker, normalize factor scales during training, and absorb the inverse
117   scales into the core. This gauge fixing does not change the latent tensor S or the model class, but it
118   makes the alternating optimization more stable in the low-rank regimes used in the experiments.

119   4     Theory
120   The analysis formalizes the mechanism behind NTD-PL: polynomial links preserve the low-rank
121   Tucker latent tensor while inducing structured high-order interactions in the observed tensor. The
122   results below are representation results. They explain what the model class can express under a shared
123   entrywise observation mechanism; they do not assert identifiability or guarantee that a nonconvex
124   optimizer recovers the representing parameters.

163   5     Optimization
164   The objective in Section 3 is nonconvex in the Tucker core, factors, and link coefficients jointly, but it
165   has a useful block structure. With the polynomial link fixed, the loss can be differentiated through
166   the Tucker reconstruction. With the latent tensor fixed, the link coefficients solve a low-dimensional
167   ridge regression on the currently observed entries. Algorithm 1 summarizes the solver used in the
168   experiments.
169   Unlike a post-hoc calibration baseline, the Tucker tensor is not held fixed after initialization. The link
170   update uses the current latent tensor, and the subsequent backbone gradient step is taken under the
171   current nonlinear link. This coupling is the optimization counterpart of the model distinction made in
172   Section 3.

191   The polynomial family is nested in the maximum degree, so we use a continuation schedule that
192   starts from the Tucker-compatible degree-one model and activates one additional degree at a time:

193   When degree p + 1 is activated, the previous coefficients and Tucker factors are kept and the new
194   coefficient βp+1 is initialized at zero. The model therefore expands from a linear low-rank fit instead
195   of starting from a high-degree polynomial link immediately.
196   This staging is useful because high powers of S are sensitive to scale and can dominate early gradients.
197   We also normalize factor columns during training and absorb their scales into the core; this is a
198   gauge fixing of the Tucker parameterization and does not change S. For numerical stability in the
199   ridge update, the implementation may solve the Vandermonde system on a centered and scaled latent
200   variable (S − µ)/σ, then convert the coefficients back to the original power basis of S. This changes
201   only the conditioning of the coefficient solve, not the model being optimized.

202   5.4   Cost

203   The dominant cost is forming the Tucker reconstruction and its reverse contractions on the observed
204   tensor. For dense tensors this is comparable to standard Tucker optimization at the same rank,
205   plus O(P |Ω|) work to evaluate the scalar polynomial and its derivative. The link update forms a
206   Vandermonde design or the corresponding moment equations and solves a (P + 1) × (P + 1) linear
207   system, with direct cost O(P 2 |Ω| + P 3 ). Since P is small, this cost is negligible compared with the
208   Tucker contractions. Thus the polynomial link changes the observation model without introducing a
209   tensor-valued high-order parameter block.

210   6     Experiments

211   The empirical study tests a scoped mechanism: when a Tucker backbone is kept low-rank, can a
212   shared polynomial link capture residual structure that is invisible to a linear Tucker observation
213   model? The experiments are organized to separate this question from a broad benchmark claim.
214   Controlled tensors isolate when the link should help, masked CAVE completion tests whether the
215   learned low-rank geometry generalizes to held-out entries, CAVE reconstruction ablations rule out
216   simple rank-lift and post-hoc calibration explanations, and external HSI scenes probe transfer and
217   boundary cases.

218   Protocol. Unless stated otherwise, real tensors are globally max-normalized and used without
219   spatial downsampling or cropping. NTD-PL is compared with Tucker at the same multilinear rank,
220   except in the explicit rank-lift ablation where Tucker is given extra rank. We report RMSE and
221   spectral angle (SAM), with NMSE(dB) used for cross-domain reconstruction where scale varies
222   across tensors. Completion masks entries uniformly at random and computes all completion metrics
223   only on the held-out entries.

242   6.3   CAVE Reconstruction Mechanism Checks

243   Full-observation reconstruction is a controlled mechanism check rather than the main generalization
244   test: with all entries observed, we can ask whether the polynomial link consistently removes residual
245   structure left by a matched-rank Tucker backbone. Table 2 supports this interpretation. NTD-PL
246   improves both intensity error and spectral angle across the rank range, while the advantage becomes
247   smaller as the Tucker rank increases. This trend is the expected signature of a lightweight observation
248   model: it helps most when the linear backbone is tight, and becomes less necessary as ordinary
249   multilinear capacity grows.
250   Two ablations clarify what the gain means. First, Table 3 asks how much spatial Tucker rank is
251   needed before ordinary Tucker first matches the RMSE of a lower-rank NTD-PL model, while
252   holding the spectral rank fixed. Tucker can buy back part of the RMSE gap by increasing rank, so
253   NTD-PL should not be read as a replacement for rank selection. The spectral-angle gap, however,
254   remains visible at the first RMSE-matching Tucker ranks, suggesting that the link is not merely
255   trading for a slightly larger multilinear model. Second, post-hoc scalar calibration of a fitted Tucker
256   reconstruction improves Tucker but remains below joint fitting in RMSE and SAM; the comparison
257   and implementation details are given in Appendix B.3.

258   6.4   External HSI Validity and Boundary Cases

259   Table 4 asks whether the CAVE mechanism transfers to other hyperspectral tensors and where the
260   shared-link assumption weakens. NTD-PL improves reconstruction and completion on Jasper Ridge,
261   Samson, and Urban. Cuprite is the boundary case: Tucker is already strong, leaving little coherent
262   residual for a shared polynomial link. Thus the evidence supports a scoped claim: NTD-PL is most

265   7     Limitations
266   NTD-PL assumes that the residual left by a low-rank Tucker backbone is well described by a shared
267   entry-level nonlinear response. It is therefore not a universal tensor model: when Tucker already fits
268   well at the chosen rank, or when residual nonlinearities vary strongly across space, spectra, or modes,
269   a single scalar link may add little.
270   The method also does not remove rank selection. Increasing Tucker rank can recover part of the
271   RMSE gap, and NTD-PL inherits Tucker’s nonconvexity and initialization sensitivity. Although the
272   link adds few parameters, evaluating it over all observed entries still increases runtime relative to
273   plain Tucker.

274   8     Conclusion
275   We introduced NTD-PL, a Tucker model with a shared polynomial observation link. Rather than re-
276   placing multilinear low-rank structure, NTD-PL adds a small nonlinear layer that expresses structured
277   residuals left by a linear Tucker fit. The theory explains this as tied high-order Tucker interactions, or
278   a structured rank lift without an independent high-rank correction.
279   The experiments support the same scoped interpretation. Controlled tensors, held-out CAVE com-
280   pletion, reconstruction mechanism checks, and external HSI scenes show that the link helps when
281   a shared nonlinear residual is plausible under a tight rank budget, while also revealing near-linear
282   boundary cases. Adaptive links and broader tensor modalities are natural next steps.
