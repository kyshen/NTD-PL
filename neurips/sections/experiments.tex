\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section asks three questions: (i) Does NTD-PL behave as expected under
controlled nonlinear residuals? (ii) Does the polynomial link enhance a Tucker
backbone in reconstruction and held-out completion? (iii) Can we diagnose when
the enhancement appears across datasets? Unless stated otherwise, real tensors
are globally max-normalized and used without spatial downsampling or cropping.
RMSE and SAM are used for reconstruction; RMSE$^\ast$ and SAM$^\ast$ are
computed only on held-out missing entries for completion. NTD-PL is compared
mainly against Tucker at the same multilinear rank, except in the explicit
rank-lift ablation. We also use a Link Refresh diagnostic score,
\(D_{\mathrm{link}}\): after fitting Tucker, run the cheap scalar Link Refresh
ridge solve once on the Tucker latent tensor, and measure the residual
reduction on task-specific evaluation entries. Larger \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\)
means the Tucker residual is more compatible with a shared nonlinear link.

\subsection{Controlled Nonlinear Residuals}
\label{subsec:controlled-experiments}

This section isolates the mechanism on synthetic tensors. We generate
observations as
\[
Y_\alpha=\sqrt{1-\alpha}S^\star+\sqrt{\alpha}\widetilde{R}^\star,
\]
where \(S^\star\) is a low-rank Tucker tensor and \(\widetilde{R}^\star\) is
the normalized component of a scalar nonlinear response orthogonal to
\(S^\star\). Thus \(\alpha\) controls nonlinear residual energy rather than the
raw amplitude of a scalar nonlinearity. When the source is linear or nearly
linear, NTD-PL should collapse toward Tucker-like behavior; as nonlinear
residual energy increases, it should separate from linear tensor baselines.
The alpha sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-alpha-grid} shows this
transition, while the degree-gain heatmap in
Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-pmax-strip} ties the gain to the expressive order
of the polynomial link. Degree continuation and coefficient-contribution
details are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:controlled-synthetic}, which also
reports the linear consistency negative control.

\subsection{Tucker Enhancement on Real Hyperspectral Tensors}
\label{subsec:lowrank-completion}

This section asks whether NTD-PL enhances a Tucker backbone on real
hyperspectral tensors, both when all entries are observed and when evaluation
is performed on held-out missing entries.

\paragraph{Representation enhancement.}
Full-observation reconstruction is a representation-capacity check, not a
generalization test. Figure~\ref{fig:cave-reconstruction-capacity} shows that
NTD-PL improves both RMSE and SAM on CAVE at matched Tucker backbone rank and
similar parameter budget. The advantage shrinks as Tucker rank increases,
which is expected: ordinary multilinear capacity already explains more of the
tensor. The rank-lifted Tucker ablation in Table~\ref{tab:rank-lift-tucker}
clarifies the mechanism further. Tucker can buy back part of the RMSE gap by
spending more rank, so the gain is not explained by a small ordinary rank
increase alone. They support the view that NTD-PL improves a tight Tucker
backbone through a jointly fitted shared link. The joint link-family smoke test
in Appendix~\ref{app:joint-link-family} further suggests that the effect is
tied to joint scalar-link fitting rather than to the polynomial basis alone.

\paragraph{Held-out enhancement.}
Masked completion is the stronger test because it asks whether the learned
low-rank geometry transfers to unobserved entries. Table~
\ref{tab:lowrank-completion-main} reports CAVE completion at several random
mask rates, with RMSE$^\ast$ and SAM$^\ast$ computed only on missing entries.
Under the same rank budget as Tucker, NTD-PL gives consistent held-out RMSE$^\ast$
gains and usually improves SAM$^\ast$ as well. The paired sign and Wilcoxon
tests in Appendix~\ref{app:single-rank-completion} show the same pattern
scene by scene.

\subsection{Diagnosing Nonlinear Low-Rank Structure Across Data}
\label{subsec:external-hsi-validity}

NTD-PL is not expected to help uniformly on all tensors. Its gain should appear
when a matched-rank Tucker fit leaves a coherent residual that a shared scalar
link can reduce. We denote this diagnostic by \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\): after
fitting Tucker, we freeze the Tucker backbone, run one scalar Link Refresh
ridge solve, and measure the residual-energy reduction on the relevant
evaluation entries. Larger \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) indicates a stronger shared
entry-level nonlinear residual:
\[
D_{\mathrm{link}}
=
10\log_{10}
\frac{
  \|Y_{\mathrm{eval}}-\widehat{Y}_{T}\|_F^2
}{
  \|Y_{\mathrm{eval}}-\widehat{Y}_{\mathrm{link}}\|_F^2
},
\]
where \(\widehat{Y}_{T}\) is the Tucker output and
\(\widehat{Y}_{\mathrm{link}}\) is the output after the one-step refresh.

For HSI, we use natural scene-level tensor units rather than artificial
patches. CAVE already provides multiple scene tensors, so
Figure~\ref{fig:cave-link-yield} evaluates the scene-level relationship
between \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) and NTD-PL held-out RMSE$^\ast$ gain.

  RMSE$^\ast$ gain versus Link Refresh diagnostic \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\). The
  left and right panels use \(\rho=0.3\) and \(\rho=0.5\), respectively, and

Figure~\ref{fig:cave-link-yield} gives the scene-level diagnostic on CAVE
completion, where higher \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) is associated with larger
held-out RMSE$^\ast$ gains. We next use the same diagnostic on external HSI
scenes. Here each dataset is treated as a whole HSI cube rather than split
into patches, because the goal is to test whether a shared scalar link is
coherent for the entire observed scene. Table~\ref{tab:real-hsi-robustness}
summarizes the resulting diagnosis. Jasper Ridge and Samson have positive
\(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) and show clear NTD-PL gains; Urban gives a more moderate
gain; Cuprite has nearly zero \(D_{\mathrm{link}}\) and is therefore a
predicted boundary case rather than a failure case.
